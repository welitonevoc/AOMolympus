About Constraints

 

Constraints are critical for making sure your object groups and sub-areas aren't placed in the wrong spot. For example, you don't want a relic in the middle of a forest sub-area, or a cliffy hill overlapping a lake, or two settlements clumped together. Constraining where resources and obstacles are placed makes maps fairer and more playable. Constraints can be used for both sub-areas and object groups within a grid square class. Even though object groups and sub-areas are only placed in the class you created them in, their constraints will keep them away from items in other areas of the map too; for example, you can make all Settlements in all areas avoid Settlement objects by 40 tiles. This ensures that the map is fair throughout.

Note that Constraints only apply in relation to objects and terrains that have already been placed on the map. Read the Placement Order section for information on how map features are generated.

Each Constraint has a constraint type, a distance in tiles, and a name of objects/terrains to avoid or stay near to. Here's what the different constraint types do:

  • AvoidSubArea: Enter the name of a sub-area within the same class and the item placed will stay away from it.
  • AvoidObjectType: Enter the proto-name of an object or a generic name such as "Gold" or "Herdable" (use the drop-down list for help), and the item will stay away from it.
  • AvoidPassableTerrain: Stay away from passable land or water (use the "name" property to choose which). For example, you want fish to stay away from passable land.
  • AvoidImpassableTerrain: Stay away from impassable land or water (use the "name" property to choose which). For example, you may want Settlements to stay away from impassable land.
  • NearPassableTerrain: Stay within a distance of passable water or land. For example, you may want crocodiles to be placed near the beach of a "watering hole".
  • NearImpassableTerrain: Stay within a distance of impassable water or land. For example, you may want gold mines to be placed near cliff-sides in a mountainous area.

Note that often it's best to use several constraints on the same object group or sub-area. The ones you generally want are to avoid objects/sub-areas of the same type by about 15 tiles, to avoid "all" objects by about 3 tiles, and to avoid impassable land by 3 or so tiles.
